# Hiring-manager review: Senior Analyst, Pricing and Business Analytics

## 1. Screen decision

**No.** The resume shows no pricing work at all: no pricing models, pricing tests, discount or promo analysis, or margin work. This role exists to "lead Pricing analytics" and asks for "deep pricing experience."

**Reads as:** a mid-level marketing analyst. They are the sole analyst on a small team, work mostly on email and paid-social reporting, and analyse tests that others design. Their total time in analytics roles since 2019 is unclear because the date they moved from coordinator to analyst isn't given.

## 2. First impression (top third)

**What I know:**
- They are based in Pittsburgh, which works for a US-remote role.
- They are the only analyst on a nine-person marketing team at an online home-goods retailer (D2C e-commerce, but not subscription).
- They write SQL in BigQuery daily.
- They built Looker dashboards.
- They analyse A/B test results.
- They segmented win-back customers by purchase history and spend.

**Must-haves still not evidenced:**
- **Pricing analytics:** no pricing models or pricing A/B tests.
- **Advanced SQL including creating tables or schemas:** "writes SQL" doesn't show this.
- **Presenting to senior executives:** the marketing director reviewing a dashboard is not an executive presentation.
- **Using AI to speed up or QA analysis, or to build automations:** nothing at all.
- **Experimentation best practices:** only partly shown, through the traffic check.

The candidate can only close these gaps by adding real experience, not by rewording.

## 4. Fixes

1. **"Clean exports in Python notebooks with pandas a few times a month."**
   Restore the candidate's "maybe" before "a few times a month," or drop the frequency and keep "Clean data exports in Python notebooks (pandas)."
2. **"Analyze about 12 A/B tests in the last year: check whether there is enough traffic, read the results, and write up what we found."**
   - Put it in past tense and date the period so it doesn't go stale, for example "Analyzed about 12 A/B tests from Oct 2025 to Oct 2026."
   - Replace "what we found" with "findings."
3. **"Marketing Coordinator, then Marketing Analyst | Mar 2019 - Present"**
   Add the month and year of the move to Marketing Analyst so a reader can see how long they've held the analyst title rather than guessing.
4. **"Portfolio: <<LINK_WEB>> (write-ups of two tests)"**
   Move the link to the contact line, where it was in the original. A link isn't a job accomplishment, and it's easy to miss buried under one employer.
5. **"Consistently exceeded sales targets."**
   This comes only from the old resume and would read the same on anyone's resume. Add how often and by how much if the candidate can stand behind a number; otherwise cut it.

Nothing echoes the job description's wording. The rest of the lines are accurate and would read fine sent to a similar role elsewhere.

## 5. Claim-defence questions

1. **"For the win-back segments, what cut-offs and how many groups did you use, and how do you know the 2.1% to 2.5% rise came from segmentation rather than the new copy or the season? Was there a holdout?"**
   Tests their own part versus the email manager's, and whether the result was measured with a control.
2. **"Walk me through how you decide a test has enough traffic: baseline rate, minimum detectable effect, power, significance level. What do you do when a test comes back underpowered?"**
   Tests whether the traffic check reflects real experimentation practice.
3. **"Of the roughly 12 tests, pick one where your write-up changed what the team did. What did you recommend, and what happened next?"**
   Tests whether they drive decisions or only report results. This role expects them to propose tests and next steps.
4. **"In the Looker work, did you write the LookML and underlying BigQuery tables or views yourself, or build on models someone else set up?"**
   Tests the advanced-SQL and table-creation requirement behind "builds Looker dashboards."
5. **"You exceeded sales targets consistently. What were the targets, by how much did you beat them, and in how many of the five years?"**
   Tests whether an unquantified claim holds up.
