# Hiring-manager review: Senior Analyst, Pricing and Business Analytics

## 1. Screen decision

**No.** The resume shows no pricing work at all: no pricing models, no pricing experiments, no Finance or Product analysis. Pricing is the core of this role and the first listed qualification.

**Seniority:** a mid-level marketing analyst, about four years in an analyst title. They are the sole analyst on a small team, they analyze tests other people choose, and they present to one director. It does not read as a senior individual contributor who leads an area or presents to executives.

## 2. First impression (top third)

**What I know:**
- They are the only analyst on a nine-person marketing team.
- They write SQL in BigQuery daily, build Looker dashboards and analyze A/B tests.
- They use GA4, Excel, pandas, and ChatGPT for SQL help.
- They are based in Pittsburgh, so remote US works.

**Must-haves still not evidenced:**
- 2–5 years of pricing analytics, including pricing models and pricing A/B tests
- Creating tables or schemas ad hoc (only saved views)
- Presenting to senior executives (only the marketing director and team)
- AI used to QA analysis or build automations (only occasional query fixing)
- Partnership with Product or Finance, and KPI reporting beyond marketing channels

**Evidenced:** A/B test analysis, Looker, SQL (depth unclear), and a Bachelor's degree.

No edit can close the pricing gap. Pricing language should not be added.

## 4. Fixes

1. **"Conversion went from 2.1% to 2.5% over the next quarter; the email manager wrote separate emails for each group."**
   - Restore the scope: write "win-back email conversion went from 2.1% to 2.5% over the next quarter."
   - Merge it with the SQL line above so the result sits next to the work. Keep the credit to the email manager for the copy.

2. **"Wrote SQL to split customers who hadn't bought in a year by what they'd bought and how much they'd spent."**
   - Add the starting point: these lapsed customers had all been getting the same win-back email.
   - Without that, the reader doesn't know what the segmentation was for.

3. **"Analyze about 12 A/B tests in the last year: check for enough traffic, read results, and write up findings. The email manager or paid social lead chooses what to test."**
   - Replace "in the last year" with a dated span, e.g. "Oct 2025 – Oct 2026", so it doesn't go stale. Keep "about".
   - Make the tense consistent with the other bullets.

4. **"The dashboard showed $2.4M in email-channel revenue last year."**
   - On its own line it states no contribution and invites a "did you own that budget?" question.
   - Either fold it into the Looker line as scope, e.g. "email (about $2.4M revenue last year)", or cut it.

5. **"Presented to the marketing director and team."**
   - If what was presented was the test write-ups, merge it into the A/B line. Otherwise cut it.
   - As a standalone bullet it reads thin and highlights the audience gap.

6. **"Use GA4 most days and sometimes use ChatGPT to help fix a SQL query."** and **"Clean exports in Python notebooks with pandas, maybe a few times a month."**
   - Both repeat the Skills line. Combine them into one tools bullet with the qualifiers kept, or rely on Skills alone.

7. **"Created a few saved views in BigQuery."**
   - Fold it into a SQL bullet (e.g. alongside the segmentation work) rather than leaving it as a near-empty line.

8. **"Portfolio: <<LINK_WEB>> (write-ups of two tests)"**
   - Move the link back to the contact line, where the original had it. A hiring manager looks for links there, not in the last bullet of a job.

## 5. Claim-defence questions

1. **"The win-back emails went from 2.1% to 2.5%. What was the baseline period and audience, and was there a holdout? How do you separate your segmentation's effect from the new copy?"**
   Tests measurement rigor and whether the candidate claims only their own part.

2. **"Walk me through how you check whether a test has enough traffic. What inputs do you use, and how do you pick a minimum detectable effect?"**
   Tests real experimentation knowledge, which is the closest bridge to pricing A/B tests.

3. **"Pick one of the ~12 tests that was inconclusive or surprised you. What did you write up, and what did you recommend?"**
   Tests whether they synthesize and recommend, or only report results on tests others designed.

4. **"What's behind the Looker dashboards the director reviews on Mondays? Did you write LookML or explores, or build on existing ones?"**
   Tests the depth of the BI build versus assembling tiles.

5. **"Show me the most complex query behind the segmentation. Have you ever needed a table you couldn't create, and what did you do?"**
   Tests whether "advanced SQL" holds up, and the gap on creating tables and schemas.
