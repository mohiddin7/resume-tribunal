### 4. Fixes

1. `"Marketing Analyst and the only analyst on a marketing team of nine at an online home-goods retailer. Write SQL in BigQuery most days, analyze the team's A/B tests and build its Looker dashboards."`
   Change "build its Looker dashboards" to "build its email and paid social Looker dashboards" so the scope accurately reflects the specific channels built rather than claiming all team dashboards.

2. `"- Analyze the marketing team's A/B tests: check there is enough traffic before a test runs, read the results and write up the findings. About 12 tests in roughly the year to Oct 2026, two written up at <<LINK_WEB>>."`
   Change "About 12 tests in roughly the year to Oct 2026" to "About 12 tests over a recent 12-month period" to avoid anchoring the bullet to an awkward, perishable calendar date.

3. `"- Made a few saved views in BigQuery; sometimes ask ChatGPT to help fix a SQL query."`
   Cut "; sometimes ask ChatGPT to help fix a SQL query" because advertising a need for ChatGPT to troubleshoot SQL syntax undermines technical credibility and reads oddly on a professional resume.

4. `"- Clean up data exports with pandas in Python notebooks, maybe a few times a month."`
   Change "maybe a few times a month" to "periodically" (or "a few times a month") to remove casual spoken phrasing while preserving the accurate frequency qualifier.

5. `"- Win-back email conversion rose from 2.1% to 2.5% the next quarter; the email manager wrote a different email per group."`
   Change "; the email manager wrote a different email per group" to "; partnered with the email manager who wrote tailored copy per segment" to present cross-functional collaboration professionally rather than attributing execution to an external party.

6. `"- Worked mostly on social posting, plus the weekly reporting in Google Analytics."`
   Rephrase to "Managed social media posting and delivered weekly channel reporting in Google Analytics" to replace conversational phrasing with professional resume language while maintaining scope.

1. **Question:** "On the win-back campaign, what specific SQL logic and purchase thresholds did you use in BigQuery to segment inactive customers, and how did you measure and isolate the conversion lift attributable to your segmentation versus the email manager's revised copy?"
   *Testing:* Tests segmentation rigor, quantitative methodology, and the ability to attribute impact between data targeting and creative messaging.

2. **Question:** "When checking whether there is sufficient traffic before an A/B test runs, what statistical criteria, sample size formulas, or minimum detectable effect (MDE) parameters do you calculate, and how do you determine statistical significance when reading results?"
   *Testing:* Tests the candidate's technical grasp of statistical experimentation principles versus merely eyeballing traffic volume.

3. **Question:** "For the Looker dashboards you built for email and paid social, how did you model the underlying data in BigQuery or LookML, what core KPIs were tracked, and how did executive leadership utilize those insights to change business decisions?"
   *Testing:* Tests the depth of data architecture and LookML capability versus basic report creation, as well as business acumen in connecting dashboards to leadership outcomes.

4. **Question:** "Beyond creating saved views in BigQuery, what experience do you have defining data tables or building automated transformation models, and what complex SQL challenges have required you to seek external assistance?"
