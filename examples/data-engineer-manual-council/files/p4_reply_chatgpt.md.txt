Interpreting the top third as the summary, skills and beginning of experience, I see an AWS-focused candidate with Glue ETL experience, a claimed 10TB integration workload, earlier dashboard work, an AWS certification and a master’s degree expected in December 2026. Phoenix establishes a stated US location. The current role is student IT support.

Large-volume ETL has a concrete supporting claim. Production reliability, late-arriving data, first-party product models and downstream ML use remain weaker areas of alignment.

## 4. Fixes, in priority order

1. **“Data engineering: ETL processes, data pipelines, data modeling, data warehousing, database design, relational databases, Spark, Apache Airflow, Snowflake”**  
   Retain tools the candidate can defend and add concrete experience evidence for Spark, orchestration and model design wherever it actually exists, without inferring PySpark from Glue or adding unearned job keywords.

2. **“Student Technical Support Assistant (IT) | Oct 2025 - Present”**  
   Add a concise factual description of the work or move this bare entry below the relevant engineering experience so the first role does not leave the reader guessing about current responsibilities.

3. **“Established a data governance framework with data quality checks and compliance measures. Data accuracy improved by 25%, and 100% of datasets met regulatory requirements across the organization.”**  
   Specify the candidate’s actual ownership, the accuracy measurement, the dataset population and applicable requirements, retaining the broad scope and percentages only if those details support them.

4. **“Implemented business intelligence frameworks in Tableau and Power BI, with interactive dashboards and reports for key stakeholders.”**  
   **“Those frameworks drove a 30% increase in data-driven decision-making across critical business operations.”**  
   Merge these into one concrete accomplishment, restore the original’s architecture contribution if accurate, and replace or remove the vague 30% result unless its metric and attribution can be explained.

5. **“Built a Tableau dashboard for a major airline, turning customer satisfaction data into interactive visualizations that improved service strategies.”**  
   Clarify whether this was commissioned airline work, coursework or an independent dataset project, and retain the service-strategy impact only if actual users adopted the findings.

6. **“Implemented AWS VPC for secure data transmission. Network performance improved by 25%.”**  
   Identify the actual security controls and measured network outcome, since naming a VPC alone does not explain either secure transmission or the performance improvement.

7. **“Analyzed historical 1980-2022 stock price data for a large tech company with time series models (ARMA, ARIMA, SARIMA) and improved stock trend forecasting by 15%.”**  
   Clarify whether the company was a client or the subject of the dataset, and define the forecast target, baseline, evaluation metric and held-out period behind the 15% improvement.

8. **“Master of Data Science | Private university, USA | GPA 3.7/4.0 | Expected Dec 2026”**  
   Keep “Expected Dec 2026” and add any completed qualifying undergraduate degree, if held, so the reader can assess the degree requirement.

I found no conspicuous copying of Samsara-specific wording. The main presentation issue is repeated broad impact language—“analytics efficiency,” “organizational efficiency” and “response efficiency”—without definitions that make the percentages meaningful.

## 5. Claim-defence questions

1. **You say you architected the enterprise data lake: which design decisions were yours, what did other engineers own, and what were the grains, keys and update patterns of the main tables?**  
   Tests individual ownership and actual data modeling depth.

2. **Was “over 10TB” a cumulative migration, stored dataset size or recurring processing volume, and which engines and orchestration tools ran the pipelines?**  
   Tests scale, hands-on Spark/Airflow experience and whether the volume represents ongoing production work.

3. **What specifically improved by 40% in “analytics efficiency,” what was the before-and-after measurement, and how did you isolate the effect of your change?**  
   Tests whether the headline optimization has a reproducible technical result.

4. **How did you calculate the 25% accuracy improvement and 100% regulatory compliance, and who validated the dataset coverage and requirements?**  
   Tests the credibility of the governance claim and its organization-wide scope.

5. **For these pipelines, what freshness or availability targets did you own, and how did you handle failures, retries, backfills and late-arriving records?**  
   Tests whether the candidate can maintain reliable production datasets at the level this role requires.
