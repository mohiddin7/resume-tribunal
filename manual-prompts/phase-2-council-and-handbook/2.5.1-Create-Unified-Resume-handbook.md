CONTEXT & OBJECTIVE:
The hiring market for roles is flooded with identical-looking, synthetically tailored resumes written by unedited AI generation tools. This has created a severe "trust deficit" among hiring managers and technical screeners, who now aggressively filter out candidates who use generic, keyword-stuffed templates or describe experiences that look like copied tutorial code.

I need you to act as an Elite Technical Recruiter and Engineering Sourcing Specialist. Perform an exhaustive, deep-dive research scan of developer communities (such as Reddit: r/cscareerquestions, r/EngineeringResumes, and specialized domain subreddits) and anonymous professional platforms (like Blind), as well as modern production job boards (like Greenhouse, Drata, Anthropic, or Stripe). Gather raw, highly specific intelligence on what distinguishes a highly credible, production-grade, and organically written resume from a low-fidelity, simulated profile for this specific role.

REQUISITE RESEARCH PILLARS:

1. THE TRUST DEFICIT & SCREENING VETTING
Detail the exact reasons why hiring managers are skeptical of applicants for this role. What are the telltale signs of a "faked" or "inflated" resume? Identify the specific "gatekeeper screens" (recruiter vs. hiring manager vs. senior engineer) and how their reading modes differ.
2. CLICHÉS AND TUTORIAL TRAPS
What is this role's equivalent of "built a basic LangChain API wrapper"? List the exact projects, architecture choices, and tools that instantly signal to a reviewer that the candidate only has tutorial-level or classroom experience.
3. PRODUCTION & SYSTEMS-LEVEL EXECUTION SIGNALS
What are the high-signal, "under-the-hood" engineering decisions that prove a candidate has actually shipped systems in production? Identify the critical performance metrics (e.g., latency, cost, data scaling, throughput, drift, or evaluation paradigms) that are crucial for this role, and provide the exact mathematical or statistical formulations of those metrics using standard LaTeX formatting (e.g., $Recall@k$, $MAPE$, or $F_1$).
4. TECHNICAL SKILLS GRID TAXONOMY
Determine how to structure and categorize the technical skills section for this role. What skills or tools should be grouped together, and which basic concepts or office suites (e.g., "Agile", "REST APIs", "Git", "Word") are considered redundant filler that must be omitted?
5. LINGUISTIC BANS & PROSE CONTROLS
Identify the specific verbs, adjectives, and filler words that scream "AI-generated" or "marketing fluff" (such as "spearheaded", "revolutionized", "cutting-edge"). Contrast this with the plain-spoken, active, and highly technical verbs preferred by senior engineering leads.
6. STRUCTURAL AND VISUAL LAYOUT STANDARDS
What are the exact visual layout standards required to clear both mechanical ATS parsers (like Workday, Greenhouse, and Lever) and a 6-second human scan? Cover margins, page budgets, section order, hyphen-vs-dash usage for dates, and the handling of external URLs.

OUTPUT FORMAT:
Provide your findings in a highly detailed, structured, and clinically precise Markdown document. Do not summarize or gloss over technical details. Focus heavily on actionable insights, specific technical tools, and metrics, ensuring that every claim is anchored to real-world engineering constraints and community hiring feedback.
