### 4. Fixes

1. `The dashboard showed $2.4M in email-channel revenue last year.`
   Cut this line entirely, as reporting a passive figure from a dashboard without candidate ownership, budget responsibility, or measurable contribution reads oddly and conveys no personal accomplishment.
2. `SQL, BigQuery, Looker, GA4, Excel, Python (pandas), A/B test analysis, customer segmentation, ChatGPT for SQL query help`
   Remove "ChatGPT for SQL query help" from the skills list, as advertising reliance on a chatbot for basic query syntax echoes the job description awkwardly and undermines perceived technical proficiency.
3. `- Use GA4 most days and sometimes use ChatGPT to help fix a SQL query.`
   Remove "and sometimes use ChatGPT to help fix a SQL query" and focus the bullet on daily GA4 traffic and performance analysis.
4. `- Presented to the marketing director and team.`
   Cut this standalone line or incorporate it into the Looker and A/B testing bullets to describe what insights were shared, as presenting to one's direct manager and peers is an expected baseline that reads awkwardly on its own.
5. `- Created a few saved views in BigQuery.`
   Cut this line or combine it with BigQuery query development, as saving a few views is too basic an ad-hoc action to stand alone as an achievement bullet.
6. `- Portfolio: <<LINK_WEB>> (write-ups of two tests)`
   Move the portfolio link into the contact header alongside the other links, rather than leaving it as a trailing bullet under employment experience.
7. `Marketing Analyst and the only analyst on a marketing team of nine. Write SQL in BigQuery most days, build Looker dashboards, and analyze A/B test results.`
   Rephrase the informal conversational phrasing ("Write SQL in BigQuery most days") into standard professional summary language highlighting analytical domain expertise and technical toolsets.
8. `- Conversion went from 2.1% to 2.5% over the next quarter; the email manager wrote separate emails for each group.`
   Combine this bullet with the preceding segmentation bullet into a single, cohesive achievement that connects the SQL customer segmentation directly to the email strategy and the resulting 2.1% to 2.5% conversion lift.

### 5. Claim-Defence Questions

1. **"On the approximately 12 A/B tests you analyzed in the past year, how did you calculate sample size and statistical power to verify 'enough traffic', and what decision rules did you use when evaluating statistical significance and trade-offs?"**
   - *Testing:* Whether the candidate possesses formal statistical experimentation knowledge and analytical independence versus merely reading automated dashboard outputs.
2. **"For the win-back customer segmentation project, what was the baseline conversion rate and sample size, how did you establish the specific purchase history and spend thresholds in SQL, and was there a randomized control holdout group to measure true incremental lift?"**
   - *Testing:* Whether the candidate understands incremental attribution, rigorous baseline measurement, and segmentation strategy, or if they are attributing a seasonal re-engagement lift to an uncontrolled send.
3. **"When building the Looker dashboards for email and paid social, what underlying data modeling or metric definitions did you create, and how did you validate data integrity when reporting figures like the $2.4M channel revenue?"**
   - *Testing:* The depth of the candidate's BI architecture and data-modeling capabilities (e.g., custom SQL/LookML views versus consuming clean pre-aggregated tables) and data governance rigor.
4. **"You noted using ChatGPT to troubleshoot SQL queries; could you walk through a complex query or performance issue you encountered, how you diagnosed the logic or performance bottleneck, and how you validated the output?"**
   - *Testing:* The candidate’s true SQL proficiency, debugging independence, and whether they understand query execution plans or rely on external generative tools as a crutch for basic syntax.
