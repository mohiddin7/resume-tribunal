- **“Marketing Analyst and the only analyst on a nine-person marketing team at an online home-goods retailer, writing SQL in BigQuery most days.”** Expand the summary’s emphasis to include the supported experimentation and recurring Looker reporting responsibilities, which communicate more relevant value than SQL frequency alone.

- **“Analyze the marketing team's A/B tests: check there is enough traffic before a test runs, read the results and write up the findings. About 12 tests in the year to Oct 2026, two written up at <<LINK_WEB>>.”** Retain “about” and replace “the year to Oct 2026” with the supported “the last year” unless the candidate confirms the specific period.
- **“Clean up data exports in Python notebooks with pandas, a few times a month.”** Restore “maybe a few times a month” to preserve the candidate’s stated uncertainty about frequency.

There is no material job-description mimicry or Hungryroot-specific wording that would read oddly in an application elsewhere. The missing pricing experience is an evidence gap; relabeling marketing segmentation or email experiments as pricing work would not resolve it.

**5. Claim-defence questions**

1. **For the win-back emails, what counted as a conversion, what were the sample sizes and measurement windows, and was there a concurrent control group?**  
   Tests whether the 2.1% to 2.5% comparison is reliable and whether the candidate distinguishes observed improvement from causal impact.

2. **Which parts of the win-back project did you personally own, and how did you validate the SQL segments and their assignment to email variants?**  
   Tests individual contribution, data quality, and the boundary between analysis and campaign execution.

3. **For one of the roughly 12 tests, how did you decide there was enough traffic, choose the analysis method, and determine when to stop the test?**  
   Tests experimentation depth beyond reading platform results.

4. **Walk me through the most complex BigQuery work behind a Looker dashboard: what was the data grain, how did you handle joins, and did you create or maintain any tables?**  
   Tests advanced SQL capability and ownership of reusable reporting data.

5. **What decision changed because of your test findings or Monday dashboards, who made it, and what recommendation did you personally provide?**  
   Tests whether reporting translated into business judgment and stakeholder influence.
